# Investing Fundamentals and Wealth Accumulation

## The Power of Compound Interest
Compound interest is earning interest on both your initial principal and on accumulated interest from previous periods.
Albert Einstein famously referred to compound interest as the eighth wonder of the world.
Formula: A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt)
Starting to invest in your early 20s can result in hundreds of thousands of dollars more at retirement compared to starting in your 30s, even with smaller monthly contributions.

## Index Funds vs. Active Stock Picking
- Index Funds / ETFs: Low-cost funds that track a broad market benchmark (such as the S&P 500 or Total Stock Market Index). They offer instant diversification across hundreds of companies with low expense ratios (< 0.10%).
- Active Stock Picking: Trying to buy and sell individual stocks to beat the market. Studies show over 90% of professional fund managers fail to beat low-cost index funds over a 15-year period after accounting for fees and taxes.
- Dollar-Cost Averaging (DCA): Investing a fixed dollar amount at regular intervals (e.g., $200 every month), regardless of whether stock prices are high or low. This removes emotional market timing.

## Retirement Accounts
1. 401(k) / Employer Match: Always contribute at least enough to capture your full employer match—it is literally a 100% immediate guaranteed return.
2. Roth IRA: Funded with after-tax money, but all capital gains and withdrawals after age 59½ are 100% tax-free.
3. Traditional IRA: Contributions are tax-deductible now, but withdrawals during retirement are taxed as ordinary income.
